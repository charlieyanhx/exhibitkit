# Hypothetical and simulated performance
**ALL PERFORMANCE FIGURES IN THIS DOCUMENT ARE HYPOTHETICAL, SIMULATED RESULTS. HYPOTHETICAL PERFORMANCE RESULTS HAVE MANY INHERENT LIMITATIONS, SOME OF WHICH ARE DESCRIBED BELOW. NO REPRESENTATION IS BEING MADE THAT ANY ACCOUNT WILL OR IS LIKELY TO ACHIEVE PROFITS OR LOSSES SIMILAR TO THOSE SHOWN. IN FACT, THERE ARE FREQUENTLY SHARP DIFFERENCES BETWEEN HYPOTHETICAL PERFORMANCE RESULTS AND THE ACTUAL RESULTS SUBSEQUENTLY ACHIEVED BY ANY PARTICULAR TRADING PROGRAM.** One of the limitations of hypothetical performance results is that they are generally prepared with the benefit of hindsight. In addition, hypothetical trading does not involve financial risk, and no hypothetical trading record can completely account for the impact of financial risk in actual trading; the ability to withstand losses or to adhere to a particular program in spite of losses are material points which can also adversely affect actual results. There are numerous other factors related to the markets in general or to the implementation of any specific program which cannot be fully accounted for in the preparation of hypothetical results and all of which can adversely affect actual results.

Specifically: (a) simulated fills are assumed at the quoted bid/ask ("full cross") or midpoint ("mid") as labeled, from end-of-day or intraday quote snapshots; those quotes may not have been available in the size or at the time assumed, and no allowance is made for market impact beyond the quoted spread; (b) commissions and fees are estimated; taxes are ignored; (c) the sample period is short, includes specific episodes that may dominate results, and may not be representative of future conditions; (d) case studies are single episodes selected after the fact for illustration and are not evidence of a repeatable return; (e) Sharpe ratios are annualized from daily marks and are labeled with their basis; bootstrap intervals are estimates that depend on the block length chosen; (f) results have not been audited or independently verified; (g) the research process that produced these results tested more strategies than are reported, and the reported results inherit the selection bias of that process even where they are negative. Past performance, simulated or actual, is not indicative of future results.
